The rain had been falling on Shinjuku since noon, a steady silver hiss that turned the crosswalks into mirrors and made the neon bleed. Under the awning of a shuttered tobacco shop, she shook water from her umbrella and breathed in wet asphalt, old cigarette smoke, and the sweet char of yakitori drifting from an alley she couldn't see. A train shuddered past on the elevated tracks, rattling the loose sign above her. Her stockings were soaked to the ankle, and her fingers still smelled of the convenience-store coffee she'd spilled an hour ago. When her phone buzzed against her hip, she almost ignored it. Then she saw the number glowing on the cracked screen, one she'd deleted three years ago and still knew by heart. The message was four words long: *Don't take the train.*
