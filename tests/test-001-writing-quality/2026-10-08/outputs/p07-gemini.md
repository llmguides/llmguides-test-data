Think of DNS as the contacts list on your smartphone.

You don’t memorize the 10-digit phone numbers of all your friends; you just tap their name, and your phone quietly dials the actual number behind the scenes.

The internet works the exact same way. Computers and web servers don’t communicate using human-friendly names like `YourCompany.com`—they use long strings of numbers called IP addresses (like `192.0.2.1`). When a customer types your website name into their browser, the **Domain Name System (DNS)** instantly looks up that name in the internet’s address book, finds the matching number for your server, and connects the visitor to your page.

**Why you should care:**
Whenever you launch a rebrand, switch to a new web hosting platform, or set up marketing landing pages, your DNS settings have to be updated to point to the new location. If those settings are misconfigured, your website goes completely dark, paid ad traffic lands on error pages, and your marketing campaigns instantly grind to a halt.
