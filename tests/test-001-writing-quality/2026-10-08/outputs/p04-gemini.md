The autumn deluge turned Shibuya’s neon glare into bleeding ribbons of magenta and bruised violet across the black asphalt. Rain hammered the transparent vinyl of his umbrella with the hollow rattle of dropped beads, drumming out the hiss of idling taxis and the muffled chime of the Yamanote line overhead. The air smelled of wet gravel, ozone, and the roasted-garlic vapor drifting from a basement ramen counter. He tucked his chin into the damp wool of his coat, breath misting white against the chill, when the phone in his breast pocket gave a sharp, double-pulse vibration. Sheltering the screen beneath the curved plastic, he tapped it awake, squinting against the blue glare. It was an unknown number, bearing three words that instantly shattered three years of deliberate silence.
