Think of DNS as your phone's contacts list for the internet.

When you want to call a colleague, you tap their name, and your phone looks up the number behind it. You never have to memorize the digits. Websites work the same way. Every website actually lives at a numeric address, something like a phone number for the computer that stores it. People type a friendly name like "yourcompany.com," and DNS looks up which number that name belongs to, then connects the visitor to the right place.

This lookup happens in a fraction of a second, every time someone visits your site. Your company controls its own entries, usually through whoever you bought your domain name from. Those entries say where your website lives, and also where your company email should be delivered.

**Why you should care:** if your website ever moves to a new hosting company or gets rebuilt on a new platform, someone has to update your DNS entries to point to the new location. Get that wrong or forget it, and visitors see an error page and emails may stop arriving, even though the new site itself works perfectly. So always confirm who will handle the DNS change before you plan a launch date.
