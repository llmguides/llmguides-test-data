Rain fell over Tokyo in silver sheets, blurring the neon signs of Shinjuku into trembling pools of pink and electric blue. The air smelled of wet asphalt, cigarette smoke, and the faint sweetness of roasted chestnuts from a shuttered street stall. Beneath the narrow shelter of a convenience store awning, a lone figure stood watching umbrellas bob through the crowded intersection. Thunder rolled somewhere beyond the skyscrapers, its low growl swallowed by the hiss of passing taxis. A phone vibrated inside a damp coat pocket. Once. Twice. The screen lit up with a message from an unknown number: “Don't get on the last train. I know what happened three years ago.” For a moment, the city seemed to fall silent. Then another message appeared. “And I'm standing right behind you.”
