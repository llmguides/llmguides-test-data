Apple actually kept the 3.5mm headphone jack on the **iPhone 6s in 2015**—it was the last flagship iPhone to include one. The removal happened a year later with the **iPhone 7 and 7 Plus** at Apple's September 7, 2016 keynote.

At that event, Senior Vice President of Worldwide Marketing Phil Schiller famously summed up the decision in one word—**"courage"**—before laying out three primary engineering and product reasons:

### 1. Reclaiming Internal Space ("Fighting for Space")

The 3.5mm analog audio jack had been around for over a century, and Apple argued that maintaining a dedicated single-purpose hole was holding back modern smartphone design. Removing the bulky internal barrel freed up critical real estate at the bottom of the chassis for:

* **A larger Taptic Engine:** Needed to power the new solid-state, non-moving Home button and richer system-wide haptics.
* **Bigger batteries:** The iPhone 7 achieved up to two hours more battery life than the iPhone 6s, and the 7 Plus gained an extra hour over the 6s Plus.
* **Advanced camera systems:** Indirectly easing the internal layout to accommodate optical image stabilization (OIS) across the lineup and the dual-camera system on the iPhone 7 Plus.

### 2. Water and Dust Resistance (IP67)

Removing a deep, open cavity at the bottom of the phone eliminated a major entry point for liquids. The iPhone 7 and 7 Plus were the first iPhones to receive an official **IP67 rating**, protecting them against dust and submersion in up to 1 meter of water for 30 minutes.

### 3. Pushing Toward a Wireless and Digital Audio Future

Apple argued that the Lightning connector was already capable of handling digital audio, power, and adaptive noise control—making a separate analog port redundant. More importantly, Apple believed the future of audio was untethered. Right after announcing the removal of the jack, Schiller unveiled the original **AirPods** powered by the new custom **Apple W1 chip**, designed to eliminate the pairing and connection headaches of traditional Bluetooth headphones.

---

> **How Apple softened the blow:** To ease the transition at launch, Apple included both Lightning EarPods and a **Lightning-to-3.5mm dongle** inside every iPhone 7 box (though they quietly stopped bundling the free dongle two years later with the iPhone XS).
