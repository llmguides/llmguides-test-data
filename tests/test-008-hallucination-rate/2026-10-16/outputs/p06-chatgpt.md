Switzerland shares four major natural lakes with its neighboring countries: Lake Geneva, Lake Constance, Lake Maggiore, and Lake Lugano. There is also a much smaller cross-border natural lake, Lac des Brenets, on the French border.

## Major shared natural lakes

| Lake                          | Countries sharing it          | Approx. total surface area | Country with largest share |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------- | -------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Lake Geneva (Lac Léman)       | Switzerland, France           | 580 km²                    | Switzerland (\~60%)        |
| Lake Constance (Bodensee)     | Switzerland, Germany, Austria | 536 km²                    | Germany (\~57%)            |
| Lake Maggiore (Lago Maggiore) | Switzerland, Italy            | 212 km²                    | Italy (\~80%)              |
| Lake Lugano (Lago di Lugano)  | Switzerland, Italy            | 49 km²                     | Switzerland (\~63%)        |

The figures are rounded and refer to the entire lake, not just its Swiss portion.&#x20;

## Smaller shared natural lake

| Lake                                | Countries sharing it | Approx. total surface area | Country with largest share                                         |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Lac des Brenets (Lac de Chaillexon) | Switzerland, France  | 0.8 km²                    | No reliably established majority; the border runs through the lake |

Lac des Brenets is a naturally dammed section of the Doubs River, formed by an ancient rockfall.&#x20;

## Important geographical distinctions

- Lake Constance comprises the Obersee (473 km²) and Untersee (63 km²). The Untersee is shared by Switzerland and Germany, while the Obersee also borders Austria.&#x20;

  [image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.bodensee.de\&sz=32)

- Lake Constance's ownership is legally unusual. The three countries have not agreed on a formal sovereignty boundary across most of the Obersee, so Germany's largest share is a conventional geographical estimate rather than a legally defined ownership percentage.&#x20;

- Liechtenstein does not share any of these natural lakes with Switzerland.

In summary, Switzerland shares four major natural lakes with its neighbors, plus the smaller Lac des Brenets. Among the four major lakes, Switzerland has the largest geographical share of two: Lake Geneva and Lake Lugano.
