Element 117 (**tennessine**, symbol **Ts**) was discovered by a Russian–American collaboration led by nuclear physicist **Yuri Oganessian**. First synthesized in late 2009 and announced on **April 5, 2010** (published in *Physical Review Letters* on **April 9, 2010**), its discovery was officially confirmed on **December 30, 2015**, and its permanent name was formally approved on **November 28, 2016**.

---

## Discoverers & Organizations Involved

The IUPAC/IUPAP Joint Working Party officially credited the discovery to the **Dubna–Livermore–Oak Ridge** collaboration, supported by three additional partner institutions:

* **Joint Institute for Nuclear Research (JINR)** *(Dubna, Russia)*: Conducted the heavy-ion accelerator bombardment experiments at the Flerov Laboratory of Nuclear Reactions using the U400 cyclotron.
* **Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL)** *(Oak Ridge, Tennessee, USA)*: Synthesized, isolated, and chemically purified the rare radioactive target material.
* **Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL)** *(Livermore, California, USA)*: Partnered on the experimental design, detector instrumentation, and decay-chain data analysis.
* **Vanderbilt University** & **University of Tennessee, Knoxville** *(Tennessee, USA)*: Provided theoretical nuclear physics support, experimental collaboration, and nuclear structure expertise.
* **Research Institute of Atomic Reactors (NIIAR / RIAR)** *(Dimitrovgrad, Russia)*: Electroplated the purified target material onto a titanium backing film to withstand intense beam irradiation.

*(Note: Independent experimental verification was also carried out by an international team at the **GSI Helmholtz Centre for Heavy Ion Research** in Darmstadt, Germany, which published its confirmation on **May 1, 2014**.)*

---

## Target Element & Supplying Lab

* **Target Element:** **Berkelium-249** ($^{249}_{97}\text{Bk}$), an artificial radioactive actinide with a half-life of only about 330 days.
* **Supplying Laboratory:** **Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL)** produced roughly 22 milligrams of $^{249}\text{Bk}$ via a 250-day intense neutron irradiation campaign at its **High Flux Isotope Reactor (HFIR)**, followed by 90 days of chemical cooling and separation at its **Radiochemical Engineering Development Center (REDC)**.
* **Nuclear Synthesis Reaction:** At JINR, the $^{249}\text{Bk}$ target was bombarded for 150 days with a beam of neutron-rich **calcium-48** ($^{48}_{20}\text{Ca}$) ions to synthesize the isotopes $^{293}\text{Ts}$ and $^{294}\text{Ts}$:

$$^{249}_{97}\text{Bk} + ^{48}_{20}\text{Ca} \rightarrow ^{297}_{117}\text{Ts}^* \rightarrow ^{294}_{117}\text{Ts} + 3\,^{1}_{0}\text{n}$$

$$^{249}_{97}\text{Bk} + ^{48}_{20}\text{Ca} \rightarrow ^{297}_{117}\text{Ts}^* \rightarrow ^{293}_{117}\text{Ts} + 4\,^{1}_{0}\text{n}$$

## Exact Timeline of Discovery & Naming

* April 5, 2010: Initial Synthesis & Announcement
Following a bombardment run begun on July 27, 2009 at JINR in Dubna, the collaboration observed six decay chains of element 117 and officially submitted/announced their findings on April 5, 2010 (published in Physical Review Letters on April 9, 2010).


* May 1, 2014: Independent Verification at GSI
Researchers at the GSI Helmholtz Centre for Heavy Ion Research in Darmstadt, Germany, published independent confirmation of element 117 using another batch of ORNL-supplied berkelium-249.


* December 30, 2015: Official Discovery Confirmation
The Joint Working Party of the International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC) and the International Union of Pure and Applied Physics (IUPAP) formally recognized the discovery of element 117, assigning priority to the Dubna–Livermore–Oak Ridge collaboration.


* June 8, 2016: Proposed Name Announced for Public Review
IUPAC unveiled the proposed name "tennessine" (symbol Ts)—honoring the state of Tennessee and the contributions of ORNL, Vanderbilt University, and the University of Tennessee—initiating a mandatory five-month public review period.


* November 28, 2016: Official Name Approval
The IUPAC Bureau officially approved the permanent name tennessine (Ts), replacing the provisional systematic name ununseptium (Uus).
