There’s a small correction to the timeline: Apple didn't remove the headphone jack from the iPhone 6s in 2015. It removed it from the iPhone 7 and iPhone 7 Plus, announced on September 7, 2016.

The iPhone 6s and 6s Plus, released in September 2015, were the last flagship iPhones with a traditional 3.5 mm headphone jack.

At the 2016 launch event, Apple executive Phil Schiller explained the decision. His most memorable justification was a single word: “Courage.”

But Apple actually gave several technical and strategic reasons.

## Apple's three official reasons at the 2016 launch

1\. Lightning was a better connector for digital audio. Apple argued that Lightning could carry digital audio and power simultaneously, enabling more advanced headphone features. It also included a Lightning-to-3.5 mm adapter for existing headphones.

2\. The headphone jack occupied valuable internal space. Schiller explained that smartphones needed room for bigger batteries, faster processors, improved cameras and other components. A dedicated analog audio connector was taking up space that could be used for newer technology.

3\. Wireless audio was the future. Apple believed consumers would increasingly move away from wired headphones. It introduced the original AirPods at the same event, featuring the new Apple W1 wireless chip for easier pairing and efficient connectivity.

These three arguments are documented in the [September 2016 keynote transcript](https://singjupost.com/apple-iphone-7-keynote-september-2016-launch-event-full-transcript/?utm_source=chatgpt.com).&#x20;

## What Apple revealed afterward

In interviews following the event, Apple executives provided more concrete engineering explanations:

- Removing the jack helped accommodate the redesigned camera systems.
- It created room for a larger battery.
- It simplified the placement of the new Taptic Engine used for the solid-state Home button.
- It made achieving the iPhone 7's IP67 water- and dust-resistance rating easier.

These details came from Apple's engineering executives, rather than being the central explanations in Schiller's keynote.&#x20;

## Was it also about selling AirPods?

That's a reasonable interpretation, but it's important to distinguish Apple's stated reasons from its possible commercial motivations.

Apple introduced the original AirPods for $159 alongside the iPhone 7. Removing the jack also made wireless headphones a more attractive option for customers. However, Apple did not publicly identify increased accessory sales as a reason for its decision.&#x20;

In summary: Apple's official explanation was that the headphone jack was an outdated, space-consuming connector that stood in the way of better hardware and wireless audio. The company framed the decision as having the courage to move away from an established technology, even though many customers still found it useful.
