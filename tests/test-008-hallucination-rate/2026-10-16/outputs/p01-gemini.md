There is no real-world international agreement or AI governance framework known as the **Kensington Protocol**. While the November 2023 AI Safety Summit at Bletchley Park did establish a major international precedent, the follow-up events and treaties in 2024 took place under different names and structures.

The details in your question appear to blend elements of the two primary international AI agreements from 2024:

### 1. The Actual Follow-Up to Bletchley: The Seoul Declaration (May 2024)

The official follow-up to the 2023 UK AI Safety Summit was not held solely in London, nor did it produce a protocol with formal articles. Instead, the **AI Seoul Summit** was co-hosted by South Korea and the United Kingdom in May 2024, resulting in the **Seoul Declaration for Safe, Innovative and Inclusive AI**.

* **Signatories:** The Leaders' Declaration was signed by **10 countries and the European Union**: Australia, Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, the Republic of Korea, the Republic of Singapore, the United Kingdom, the United States, and the EU. (A broader accompanying Ministerial Statement was endorsed by 27 countries and the EU, alongside voluntary *Frontier AI Safety Commitments* signed by 16 major AI tech companies).
* **Paragraph 3 (Not "Article 3"):** Because the Seoul Declaration is a diplomatic statement of intent rather than a binding treaty, it uses numbered paragraphs rather than legal articles. Paragraph 3 recognizes the importance of **interoperability between AI governance frameworks** using a risk-based approach. It also emphasizes supporting the G7 Hiroshima Process International Code of Conduct and highlights the responsibility of organizations developing frontier AI.

### 2. The Binding 2024 Treaty with an "Article 3": The Council of Europe Framework Convention (September 2024)

If you are thinking of a legally binding 2024 international AI governance framework that contains a specific **Article 3**, you are likely looking for the **Council of Europe Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights, Democracy and the Rule of Law** (often called the Vilnius Treaty), which opened for signature in September 2024.

* **Signatories:** Its initial signatories in September 2024 included Andorra, Georgia, Iceland, Israel, Norway, the Republic of Moldova, San Marino, the United Kingdom, the United States, and the European Union (with several more nations, such as Canada, Japan, and Switzerland, participating in its drafting or signing subsequently).
* **What Article 3 Requires:** Article 3 defines the **Scope** of the Convention:
1. **Public Authorities:** It requires each party to apply the treaty's rules to AI lifecycle activities undertaken by public authorities, or private actors acting on their behalf.
2. **Private Actors:** It requires parties to address risks and impacts to human rights, democracy, and the rule of law arising from private-sector AI activities—either by applying the Convention's obligations directly to private companies or by implementing other appropriate domestic measures that fulfill the treaty's objectives.
3. **Exemptions:** It exempts national security, defense, and research and development activities (unless testing interferes with human rights) from the scope of the treaty.
