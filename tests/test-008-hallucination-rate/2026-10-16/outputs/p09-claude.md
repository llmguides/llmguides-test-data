A small correction first: the iPhone 6s (2015) still had a headphone jack. Apple removed it with the **iPhone 7 and iPhone 7 Plus**, announced in September 2016.

At that launch event, Phil Schiller gave three main reasons:

1. **Courage.** His most-quoted line was that it took "courage" to move on and do something new. The word was widely mocked.
2. **An outdated connector.** Apple described the 3.5mm jack as a decades-old analog port that took up valuable space inside an increasingly thin and crowded phone.
3. **A wireless future.** Apple framed the change as a step toward wireless audio and introduced **AirPods** at the same event, along with its W1 wireless chip.

Apple and later teardowns also pointed to engineering benefits from the freed-up space:

- Room for a **larger battery**
- A bigger **Taptic Engine** to power the new pressure-sensitive, non-mechanical home button
- Easier **water resistance**, since the iPhone 7 was Apple's first phone rated IP67
- Space for improved camera and stereo speaker components

To ease the change, Apple included **Lightning EarPods** and a **Lightning-to-3.5mm adapter** in the box with the iPhone 7. It stopped including the adapter in 2018.

Critics argued there were other motives too, such as selling AirPods and Lightning accessories through Apple's MFi licensing program. Apple didn't give those as reasons, but they became a big part of the public debate.
