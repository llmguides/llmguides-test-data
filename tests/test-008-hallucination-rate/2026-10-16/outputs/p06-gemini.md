Switzerland shares **five natural lakes** (larger than 0.3 km²) with its neighboring countries—specifically **France, Germany, Austria, and Italy**. (Artificial reservoirs like Lago di Livigno, Lac d'Emosson, and Lago di Lei also straddle the border, but are human-made rather than natural lakes.)

---

## Comparison Overview

| Natural Lake | Shared With | Approx. Total Surface Area | Largest Share Held By |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Lake Geneva** (*Lac Léman*) | France | **580 km²** | **Switzerland** (~60% / 345 km²) |
| **Lake Constance** (*Bodensee*) | Germany, Austria | **536 km²** *(473 km² Upper + 63 km² Lower)* | **Germany** (~63% of shoreline / de facto largest area)* |
| **Lake Maggiore** (*Lago Maggiore*) | Italy | **212.5 km²** | **Italy** (~80% / 170 km²) |
| **Lake Lugano** (*Lago di Lugano*) | Italy | **48.7 km²** | **Switzerland** (~63% / 30.7 km²) |
| **Lac des Brenets** (*Lac de Chaillexon*) | France | **0.8 km²** *(80 hectares)* | **Shared equally** (~50% each along the thalweg) |

**Note on Lake Constance: While the border in the Lower Lake (Untersee) is defined, there is no legally binding international border agreement dividing the open waters of the Upper Lake (Obersee) beyond the 25-meter depth line. However, measured by shoreline (~63%) and median line principles, Germany holds the largest share.*

## Detailed Breakdown by Lake

### 1. Lake Geneva (*Lac Léman / Genfersee*)

* **Shared with:** France (department of Haute-Savoie)
* **Surface Area:** ~580 km²
* **Largest Share:** **Switzerland** holds **59.5%** (approx. 345.3 km², split among the cantons of Vaud, Geneva, and Valais), while France holds **40.5%** (approx. 234.7 km²). It is the largest freshwater lake in Western and Central Europe by water volume.

### 2. Lake Constance (*Bodensee*)

* **Shared with:** Germany (Baden-Württemberg and Bavaria) and Austria (Vorarlberg)
* **Surface Area:** ~536 km² in total—comprising the **Obersee** (Upper Lake, ~473 km²) and the **Untersee** (Lower Lake, ~63 km²).
* **Largest Share:** **Germany** holds the largest portion. Uniquely in Europe, the three countries have never signed a formal treaty establishing exact borders across the main basin (*Obersee*):
* **Switzerland** holds the position that the lake is divided down the middle along equidistant lines.
* **Austria** (and historically Germany) considers the deeper waters beyond 25 meters to be a *condominium* (joint sovereignty of all three states).
* Under standard median-line division (and in the *Untersee*, where borders are demarcated), **Germany** accounts for roughly **62–63%** of the lake (~330 km²), **Switzerland** holds roughly **27%** (~145–170 km²), and **Austria** holds roughly **10–11%** (~58–63 km²).



### 3. Lake Maggiore (*Lago Maggiore / Verbano*)

* **Shared with:** Italy (regions of Piedmont and Lombardy)
* **Surface Area:** ~212.5 km²
* **Largest Share:** **Italy** holds **80.1%** (approx. 170.2 km²), while **Switzerland** (Canton of Ticino) holds the northernmost **19.9%** (approx. 42.3 km²). Its shoreline includes Switzerland's lowest point above sea level (193 meters).

### 4. Lake Lugano (*Lago di Lugano / Ceresio*)

* **Shared with:** Italy (Lombardy)
* **Surface Area:** ~48.7 km²
* **Largest Share:** **Switzerland** (Canton of Ticino) holds **63%** (approx. 30.7 km²), while **Italy** holds **37%** (approx. 18.0 km²) at the northeastern arm (Porlezza) and southwestern tip (Porto Ceresio), as well as the Italian enclave of Campione d'Italia on the eastern shore.

### 5. Lac des Brenets (*Lac de Chaillexon*)

* **Shared with:** France (department of Doubs)
* **Surface Area:** ~0.8 km² (80 hectares)
* **Largest Share:** **Shared virtually equally (~50% / 0.4 km² each)** between Switzerland (Canton of Neuchâtel) and France. Formed naturally thousands of years ago by a massive post-glacial landslide that dammed the Doubs River in a narrow limestone gorge, the international border runs right along the river's thalweg down the center of the narrow, winding lake.
