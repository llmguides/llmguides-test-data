**Launch:** Voyager 1 launched on September 5, 1977, 16 days after its twin, Voyager 2.

**Interstellar space:** It left the solar system on August 25, 2012, passing the heliopause, the boundary of charged particles that marks the outer limit of the Sun's influence.

**Instruments still operating:** Only two. Voyager 1 is now gathering data with only its magnetometer and its plasma wave subsystem. The magnetometer measures the local magnetic field. The plasma wave subsystem picks up plasma oscillations and other radio signals that let scientists infer the density and behavior of the ionized gas around the spacecraft. Each Voyager originally carried 10 sets of instruments.

**Most recent shutdown:** The Low-energy Charged Particles experiment (LECP). Engineers at JPL turned it off on April 17, 2026. The LECP had run almost continuously since launch, nearly 49 years. It measured low-energy ions, electrons and cosmic rays from our solar system and galaxy. The trigger was an unexpected drop in power during a routine roll maneuver in late February 2026. Engineers left a small half-watt motor on the LECP running in case the instrument can be used again. Before that, Voyager 1's last instrument loss was in February 2025, when engineers turned off its cosmic ray subsystem experiment.

One more milestone is coming up. On November 15, 2026, Voyager 1 is projected to reach exactly one light-day from Earth, about 16.1 billion miles (25.9 billion km).
