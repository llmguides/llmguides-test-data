Her co-inventor was **George Antheil**, an American avant-garde composer. Their idea used piano-roll-style synchronization to make a transmitter and receiver hop together across 88 frequencies, the number of keys on a piano. The goal was to make radio-guided torpedoes hard to jam.

- **Patent:** US Patent No. **2,292,387**, titled "Secret Communication System"
- **Filed:** June 10, 1941
- **Granted:** August 11, 1942
- **Name on the patent:** **Hedy Kiesler Markey**. This combined her birth surname (Kiesler) with the surname of her then-husband, Gene Markey. "Hedy Lamarr" was a stage name.

The US Navy did not adopt the system during the war. The patent expired before frequency-hopping ideas became widely used in military and later civilian communications. Lamarr and Antheil were recognized much later, including with an Electronic Frontier Foundation Pioneer Award in 1997 and induction into the National Inventors Hall of Fame in 2014.
