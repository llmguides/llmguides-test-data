Element 117, tennessine (Ts), was discovered in 2010 by a joint Russian–American research team led by nuclear physicist Yuri Oganessian at the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research (JINR) in Dubna, Russia. Its discovery was officially recognized in 2015, and its name was approved in 2016.

## How was tennessine created?

Scientists bombarded berkelium-249 (²⁴⁹Bk), an isotope of element 97, with accelerated calcium-48 (⁴⁸Ca) ions.

The nuclear fusion reaction can be represented as:

\\[ {}^{249}\_{97}\mathrm{Bk}+{}^{48}\_{20}\mathrm{Ca} \rightarrow{}^{297}\_{117}\mathrm{Ts}^{\*} \rightarrow{}^{293}\_{117}\mathrm{Ts}+4n \\]

- Target element: Berkelium (Bk), specifically berkelium-249.
- Target supplier: Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL), Tennessee, USA.
- Production facility: ORNL's High Flux Isotope Reactor, with purification at its Radiochemical Engineering Development Center.
- Bombardment facility: The U-400 cyclotron at JINR in Dubna, Russia.

Oak Ridge produced approximately 22 milligrams of berkelium-249, which was sent to Russia after target preparation arrangements. The experiments initially detected six atoms of element 117.&#x20;
