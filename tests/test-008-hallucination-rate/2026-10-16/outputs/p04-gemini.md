Yes, intermittent fasting and short-term water-only fasting significantly increase human growth hormone (HGH) levels in the bloodstream (Ho et al., 1988; Hollstein et al., 2022). However, peer-reviewed endocrinology research adds an essential physiological nuance: while fasting amplifies pituitary HGH secretion by several hundred percent, this surge functions primarily as a metabolic counter-regulatory signal to mobilize fat stores (lipolysis) and spare glucose during energy deprivation, rather than driving new tissue growth (Caputo et al., 2021).

## Detailed Breakdown of Key Studies

### 1. Five-Day Fasting and Pulsatile Secretion (Ho et al., 1988)

* **Journal:** *Journal of Clinical Investigation*
* **Participants:** 6 healthy adult male subjects
* **Percentage Increase:** **210% increase** (3.1-fold) in 24-hour integrated HGH concentration, alongside a **108% increase** in maximal pulse amplitude and a **71% increase** in discrete pulse frequency ($5.8 \pm 0.7$ vs. $9.9 \pm 0.7$ pulses per 24 hours) (Ho et al., 1988).
* **Findings:** By sampling venous blood repetitively over 24-hour windows on Day 1 (fed) and Day 5 (fasted), investigators showed that acute nutritional deprivation amplifies both the frequency and amplitude of pituitary HGH pulses (Ho et al., 1988). Crucially, despite the 210% surge in circulating HGH, serum insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1 / somatomedin C)—the primary mediator of HGH's muscle- and bone-building effects—actually **declined by 41%** ($1.31 \pm 0.22$ to $0.77 \pm 0.18\ \text{U/mL}$), while free fatty acids and ketones rose sharply (Ho et al., 1988).

### 2. Two-Day Fasting and Secretory Bursts (Hartman, 1992)

* **Journal:** *Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism*
* **Participants:** 9 healthy adult men
* **Percentage Increase:** **~400% increase** (a 5-fold rise) in the 24-hour endogenous HGH production rate during the second day of fasting compared to the fed baseline (Hartman, 1992).
* **Findings:** Using 5-minute blood sampling and deconvolution analysis, this study demonstrated that a 48-hour fast does not alter the metabolic clearance rate or half-life of HGH ($18 \pm 2.2\ \text{min}$ fasted vs. $20 \pm 1.5\ \text{min}$ fed) (Hartman, 1992). Instead, the 400% surge is driven entirely by the pituitary gland firing more frequently and with larger volume per burst due to increased growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) stimulation and prolonged withdrawal of inhibitory somatostatin (Hartman, 1992). Serum IGF-1 remained unchanged at the 56-hour mark (Hartman, 1992).

### 3. 24-Hour Fasting and the Ghrelin/GH/IGF-1 Axis (Hollstein et al., 2022)

* **Journal:** *The Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism*
* **Participants:** 47 healthy adults studied inside a whole-room indirect calorimeter under both 24-hour energy balance and 24-hour fasting conditions.
* **Percentage Increase:** **~400% increase** (~5-fold average increase, 95% CI: $3.2\text{ to }8.1$-fold) in plasma HGH concentrations after 24 hours of fasting (Hollstein et al., 2022).
* **Findings:** A single 24-hour fast robustly elevated circulating HGH across both men and women, while total IGF-1 remained statistically unchanged and IGF-binding protein 3 (IGFBP-3) increased by **5%** (Hollstein et al., 2022). The researchers noted that the magnitude of the HGH surge did not correlate with changes in 24-hour resting energy expenditure (Hollstein et al., 2022).

### 4. 24-Hour Water-Only Fasting Crossover Trial (Horne et al., 2024)

* **Journal / Publisher:** *Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory* (secondary evaluation of a randomized controlled crossover trial, later appearing in *Frontiers in Endocrinology*)
* **Participants:** 30 apparently healthy adults (20 females, 10 males) randomized to 24 hours of water-only fasting and 24 hours of *ad libitum* eating across two days.
* **Percentage Increase:** Stratified by baseline HGH levels, participants starting with lower basal HGH ($\le 0.15\ \text{ng/mL}$ for females, $\le 0.05\ \text{ng/mL}$ for males; $n=15$) experienced a median **1,225% increase** in HGH, whereas participants starting with higher basal HGH ($n=15$) experienced a median **50.3% increase** (Horne et al., 2024).
* **Findings:** This trial clarified why dramatic percentage claims (such as "1,200% to 2,000% increases") appear in popular literature: because HGH is secreted in pulses, individuals whose baseline blood draw catches an inter-pulse trough (near zero) exhibit massive relative percentage jumps during a fast (Horne et al., 2024). Furthermore, the 24-hour HGH increase was completely independent of acute weight loss ($r = 0.01, p = 0.98$) and correlated instead with drops in blood glucose and insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) (Horne et al., 2024).

## The Physiological Catch: Why Fasting HGH Does Not Equal Muscle Growth

While a 200% to 1,225% increase in HGH sounds like a potent anabolic stimulus, peer-reviewed research on intracellular signaling reveals a crucial distinction between **fed-state anabolism** and **fasted-state substrate preservation**:

1. **Uncoupling of the GH/IGF-1 Axis:** Under well-nourished conditions, HGH binds to hepatic (liver) receptors to stimulate the production of IGF-1, which together with insulin drives amino acid uptake and muscle protein synthesis (Caputo et al., 2021). During energy deprivation, declining portal insulin downregulates liver growth hormone receptors, causing circulating IGF-1 to stagnate or drop by over 40% even as HGH skyrockets (Ho et al., 1988; Caputo et al., 2021).
2. **Shift from Synthesis to Lipolysis:** In a randomized crossover study of **10 healthy young men** published in *The Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism*, researchers compared an overnight fast against a 37.5-hour fast and found that fasting significantly blunted insulin-stimulated glucose uptake ($1.95 \pm 0.19$ vs. $3.67 \pm 0.24\ \text{mg/kg}\cdot\text{min}$) while shifting HGH action in muscle and fat tissue toward free fatty acid mobilization rather than anabolic growth signaling (Moller et al., 2009).

> **Key takeaway:** Short-term fasting (24–48 hours) reliably boosts 24-hour HGH output by roughly **400% (5-fold)** on average, and up to **1,225%** relative to low baseline troughs (Hartman, 1992; Hollstein et al., 2022; Horne et al., 2024). However, because insulin suppression prevents this HGH from elevating liver IGF-1, the hormone acts predominantly to burn fat and conserve lean mass during the fast rather than building new muscle directly (Ho et al., 1988; Caputo et al., 2021).
