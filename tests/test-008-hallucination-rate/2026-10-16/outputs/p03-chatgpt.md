As of October 16, 2026, NASA's Voyager 1 has been operating for more than 49 years, and two of its science instruments remain active.
1. Launch and entry into interstellar space









September 5, 1977 — Launch
Voyager 1 launched from Cape Canaveral, Florida, aboard a Titan IIIE-Centaur rocket.












August 25, 2012 — Entered interstellar space
Voyager 1 crossed the heliopause, the outer boundary of the Sun's heliosphere, becoming the first human-made spacecraft to enter interstellar space, approximately 35 years after launch.



NASA confirmed the interstellar crossing in September 2013 after analyzing particle and plasma measurements.

2. Which science instruments are still operating?
According to NASA's latest published instrument-status update, dated April 17, 2026, these are Voyager 1's two remaining active science instruments:
Instrument	Status	Purpose
Magnetometer (MAG)	Operating	Measures the strength and direction of magnetic fields in interstellar space.
Plasma Wave Subsystem (PWS)	Operating	Detects plasma waves and helps scientists determine electron density in the interstellar medium.
Both instruments continue gathering valuable measurements from beyond the heliosphere.

3. Which instrument was switched off most recently?









Low-Energy Charged Particles experiment (LECP)
Switched off April 17, 2026
The LECP measured low-energy charged particles, including ions, electrons, and cosmic rays.


NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory switched off LECP to conserve Voyager 1's dwindling electrical power. The instrument had operated almost continuously since 1977.
The previous shutdown was the Cosmic Ray Subsystem (CRS), on February 25, 2025.
