Voyager 1 was launched on **September 5, 1977**, and crossed the heliopause to enter interstellar space on **August 25, 2012**. Out of its original 10 science instruments (11 including the spacecraft's radio science system), only **two** remain operational: the **Magnetometer (MAG)** and the **Plasma Wave Subsystem (PWS)**. The most recently deactivated instrument is the **Low-Energy Charged Particles (LECP)** detector, which NASA switched off on **April 17, 2026**, to conserve the spacecraft's dwindling electrical power.

### Mission Launch & Interstellar Crossing

* **Launch Date (September 5, 1977):** Voyager 1 lifted off from Cape Canaveral, Florida, aboard a Titan IIIE-Centaur rocket (16 days after its twin, Voyager 2). Although initially designed for a five-year mission to study Jupiter and Saturn, its trajectory carried it toward the outer boundary of the Solar System.
* **Interstellar Space Entry (August 25, 2012):** At a distance of roughly 121 AU (about 18.1 billion km / 11.2 billion miles) from the Sun, Voyager 1 crossed the **heliopause**—the boundary where the outward pressure of the Sun's solar wind gives way to the cold, dense plasma of the interstellar medium—making it the first human-made object to leave the heliosphere.

### Currently Operating Science Instruments

Notice in the diagram above how the spacecraft's instruments are distributed across several booms to prevent interference from the main bus and the Radioisotope Thermoelectric Generators. Only two instruments continue to collect and transmit science data:

1. **Magnetometer (MAG):** Mounted along the long **Extendable Magnetometer Boom**, MAG measures the strength, direction, and fluctuations of the interstellar magnetic field beyond the Sun's influence.
2. **Plasma Wave Subsystem (PWS):** Using two long V-shaped antennas (labeled **Planetary Radio Astronomy and Plasma Wave Antenna** at the bottom right of the diagram), PWS measures electron density and local wave-particle interactions in the interstellar plasma. Because Voyager 1's main Plasma Science (PLS) sensor failed in 1980, PWS was critical in confirming that the probe had entered the denser plasma of interstellar space in 2012.

### Most Recent Instrument Shutdown

* **Low-Energy Charged Particles (LECP) — Turned off April 17, 2026:** Mounted on the **Science Instrument Boom**, the LECP measured the fluxes and energy distributions of ions and electrons across the outer heliosphere and interstellar space. Because Voyager 1's plutonium-238 Radioisotope Thermoelectric Generators (RTGs) lose roughly 4 watts of electrical output per year due to radioactive decay and thermocouple degradation, engineers at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) sent commands to shut down the LECP's main sensor on April 17, 2026.
* **Potential Reactivation:** JPL engineers intentionally left the LECP's 0.5-watt stepping motor running so the sensor continues to rotate and stay warm, preserving the option to reactivate the instrument if planned power-management workarounds free up sufficient wattage. Prior to the LECP, NASA shut down the **Cosmic Ray Subsystem (CRS)** on **February 25, 2025**.
