Hedy Lamarr co-invented the frequency-hopping spread spectrum system (titled **"Secret Communication System"**) with avant-garde composer **George Antheil** to prevent radio-guided Allied torpedoes from being jammed by Axis powers.

* **Co-Inventor:** George Antheil
* **U.S. Patent Number:** **2,292,387**
* **Date Filed:** June 10, 1941 (Application Serial No. 397,412)
* **Date Granted:** August 11, 1942
* **Name Used on the Patent:** **Hedy Kiesler Markey** — her legal married name at the time, combining her maiden surname (*Kiesler*) and the last name of her second husband, screenwriter and producer Gene Markey.

### How the Patent Worked

Drawing on Antheil's experience synchronizing sixteen mechanical player pianos for his 1924 composition *Ballet Mécanique*, the pair designed a system that used matching perforated paper rolls—one inside the transmitting reconnaissance aircraft or ship and an identical one inside the torpedo—driven by synchronized clockwork motors. As the paper rolls advanced across **88 carrier frequencies** (matching the 88 keys of a standard piano), suction pins dropped through the perforations to close tuning capacitor switches in tandem, hopping the radio link from one frequency to the next before an enemy eavesdropper could lock onto or jam the signal.
