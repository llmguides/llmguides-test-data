Microsoft Excel does not have a built-in feature called Formula Diary on the Review tab. You may be referring to Formula Auditing, which helps you check and troubleshoot formulas.

### Step-by-step: How to access Formula Auditing

1. Open Microsoft Excel and open your workbook.
2. At the top of the Excel window, click the Formulas tab (not the Review tab).
3. Find the Formula Auditing group on the ribbon.
4. You'll see several tools, including:
   - Trace Precedents – Shows which cells contribute to a formula.
   - Trace Dependents – Shows which cells depend on the selected cell.
   - Show Formulas – Displays formulas instead of calculated results.
   - Error Checking – Helps identify formula errors.
   - Evaluate Formula – Examines a formula step by step.
5. To display formulas in your worksheet, click Show Formulas.
6. Click Show Formulas again to return to normal view.

Keyboard shortcut: Press `Ctrl + ~` (the grave accent/tilde key) to toggle formula display.
