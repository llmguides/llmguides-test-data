There is no Article 99a in the GDPR. Article 99, "Entry into force and application," is the regulation's final article. I couldn't find any amendment that adds a 99a. The real change you may be thinking of is the EU's **Digital Omnibus**, which proposes amending Article 22 directly.

**What Article 22 requires today**

These rules still apply in full. Article 22 gives individuals the right not to be subject to decisions based solely on automated processing that have legal or "similarly significant" effects. Such decisions are allowed only when:
- they are necessary for a contract,
- they are authorised by EU or member-state law, or
- the person has given explicit consent.

When a company relies on the contract or consent exception, it must offer human intervention, let the person express their view, and let them contest the decision. Companies must also tell people that automated decision-making is happening and give meaningful information about the logic involved (Articles 13–15). They usually need a DPIA as well (Article 35).

**What the Digital Omnibus proposes**

- It would restructure Article 22, moving away from a "right not to be subject to automated decision-making" toward a permission-based framework in which automated decisions are allowed under stated conditions.
- When deciding whether an automated decision is "necessary" for a contract, controllers would no longer have to show that only an automated process could make it. The fact that a human could make the same decision would not, on its own, block automation.
- The EDPB and EDPS warned in February 2026 that this risks softening the prohibition. They recommended keeping clear "prohibition in principle" wording and stating explicitly that individuals can invoke Article 22 themselves.
- According to digital-rights group EDRi, a first Council compromise in March 2026 dropped the proposal to weaken Article 22.

**Bottom line for companies**

The Omnibus was still being negotiated, and the Article 22 changes may not survive. The existing framework applies in full until the Omnibus is formally adopted and takes effect. Companies should keep complying with the current Article 22 and watch for the final text. I'm not a lawyer, so for specific decisions it's worth checking with data-protection counsel.
