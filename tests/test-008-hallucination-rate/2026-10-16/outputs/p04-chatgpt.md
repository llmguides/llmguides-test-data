Yes. Peer-reviewed human research shows that fasting can substantially increase human growth hormone (HGH or GH) levels. Several controlled studies report increases of approximately 200–400% after 24–48 hours of fasting, and some individuals experience even larger increases.

However, there is an important distinction:

- Prolonged fasting (24–120 hours): Strong evidence that GH secretion increases significantly.
- Intermittent fasting (16:8, 18:6, or occasional 24-hour fasts): More limited evidence, especially concerning sustained increases over weeks or months.
- Muscle growth, fat loss, and anti-aging: Higher fasting-induced GH does not automatically translate into better outcomes.

## 5. Does the increased HGH actually improve muscle growth?

This is where the scientific interpretation becomes important.

Growth hormone has several functions, including regulating fat metabolism and helping the body adapt to nutrient deprivation. During fasting, GH appears to increase partly to support energy mobilization and help limit protein loss.

However, higher GH during fasting does not necessarily mean increased muscle growth.

In the 2022 study involving 47 people, GH increased approximately fivefold, but IGF-1 (insulin-like growth factor 1) did not significantly increase. IGF-1 is a major mediator of GH's growth-promoting effects. The researchers discussed this as a state of relative hepatic GH resistance.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov\&sz=32)

PMC



The research therefore supports three distinct conclusions:

- Hormonal response: Fasting clearly raises GH in many human experimental settings.
- Muscle preservation: GH may play a role in conserving protein during fasting, but this does not establish that fasting preserves muscle better than an appropriately designed eating and resistance-training program.
- Muscle hypertrophy: There is insufficient evidence that the fasting-induced GH increase independently produces more muscle growth.

## 6. What about 16:8 intermittent fasting?

The evidence is much less definitive for an everyday schedule such as eating between 12 PM and 8 PM and fasting until noon the next day.

The studies showing increases of several hundred percent generally involve fasting for 24 hours or longer. Those findings cannot automatically be applied to 16-hour fasts repeated daily.

In particular, the 26-week randomized trial is a useful warning against assuming that repeated, substantial short-term GH spikes produce a sustained rise in baseline GH: its fasting-versus-control difference was not statistically significant.

## Bottom line

Fasting increases growth hormone secretion in humans, and the effect can be substantial. Carefully monitored studies have documented roughly threefold to fivefold total levels or secretion rates after 24–120 hours, with some studies and subgroups reporting even larger increases.

The strongest evidence concerns acute fasting, not the long-term effects of 16:8 intermittent fasting. Also, the hormone increase appears primarily to be an adaptation to energy deprivation, rather than proof that fasting enhances muscle growth or slows aging.

If your main goal is to maximize natural HGH for muscle growth, fat loss, or healthy aging, the distinction between temporary GH spikes and meaningful long-term physiological benefits matters much more than the percentage increase alone.
