Switzerland shares four natural lakes with its neighbours: Lake Geneva, Lake Constance and Lake Maggiore are large, Lake Lugano is mid-sized, and there is one small lake on the Doubs river.

| Lake | Shared with | Approx. area | Largest share |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lake Geneva** (Lac Léman) | France | ~580 km² | **Switzerland**, about 60% (~345 km² vs ~235 km² French) |
| **Lake Constance** (Bodensee) | Germany, Austria | ~536 km² (Obersee + Untersee) | **Germany**, by shoreline and by the usual estimates. There is no agreed border on most of the lake (see below). |
| **Lake Maggiore** | Italy | ~212 km² | **Italy**, about 80% (Switzerland ~42 km², in Ticino) |
| **Lake Lugano** | Italy | ~49 km² | **Switzerland**, about 63% |
| **Lac des Brenets** (Lac de Chaillexon) | France | under 1 km² | Roughly even. The border follows the Doubs through the middle of the lake. |

**Lake Constance has no legally fixed borders on most of its water.** The three countries have never agreed where the boundaries lie on the main basin, the Obersee. Austria treats it as a condominium shared by all three. Switzerland holds that the border runs down the middle of the lake. Germany has never taken a firm position. Only the Untersee and the Konstanz bay area have defined borders. So "Germany holds the largest share" is based on Germany having the longest shoreline (roughly 170 of about 273 km), not on an official division of the lake's area.

Reservoirs that straddle the border, such as Lago di Lei, are artificial, so I've left them out.
